KubeVirt CSI: guest PVCs on the underlay's lvms-vg1

Guest cluster ocp-vm-sno (OCP 5.0.1, nodes are VMs) asks the underlay ocp-sno (OCP 4.22 SNO, OpenShift Virtualization 4.22) for disks. Each guest PVC becomes a DataVolume in swongpai-virt, hot-plugged into the guest node VM that runs the pod.

2. How the disk moves when the pod moves

The data never copies. The same thin LV on the underlay is unplugged from one guest VM and plugged into another.

3. Other lifecycle operations

Guest actionGuest sidecar → driver callWhat happens on the underlay
Create PVCcsi-provisioner → CreateVolumeBlank DataVolume pvc-<uid> on lvms-vg1, labelled csi-driver/cluster=ocp-vm-sno. The PVC stays WaitForFirstConsumer until the hotplug pod uses it.
Pod startscsi-attacher → ControllerPublishVolume, then kubelet → NodeStage/NodePublishvirtualmachines/<vm>/addvolume. Hotplug pod, SCSI disk hot-attached to QEMU.
Pod stopskubelet → NodeUnpublish/NodeUnstage, csi-attacher → ControllerUnpublishVolumevirtualmachines/<vm>/removevolume. The disk is detached and the hotplug pod is deleted. The LV keeps its data.
Expand PVCcsi-resizer → ControllerExpandVolume, then NodeExpandVolumeThe underlay PVC grows (TopoLVM allowVolumeExpansion). The VM sees the new size (feature gate ExpandDisks is on), and the guest grows the filesystem.
VolumeSnapshotcsi-snapshotter → CreateSnapshotUnderlay VolumeSnapshot with class lvms-vg1 (from infraSnapshotClassName and storageSnapshotMapping).
Clone / restorecsi-provisioner → CreateVolume with a sourceDataVolume with dataSourceRef to the source PVC or snapshot. CDI clones it.
Delete PVCcsi-provisioner → DeleteVolumeThe DataVolume and PVC are deleted, and TopoLVM frees the thin LV.
